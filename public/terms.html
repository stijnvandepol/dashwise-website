5. Using Dashwise safely

  1. Driving. Set Dashwise up before you drive. Don't hold or operate your phone while driving. Use a fixed mount, or let a passenger use the app. Follow the traffic rules of the country where you drive.
  2. Warnings. No warning in Dashwise does not mean that everything is fine. Your car's own gauges and warning lights come first.
  3. Fault codes. Clearing fault codes erases stored data and resets the readiness monitors. It does not fix the cause, and a vehicle inspection may fail or need an extra test afterwards. Only clear codes when you know why they are there. Never clear codes to hide a fault from a buyer, an inspector or a warranty provider.
  4. Trips and the mileage log. Distances are estimates from GPS or the car's speed, and addresses come from Apple Maps. The mileage log is a convenience, not a tax logbook for a company car. You decide what you submit to an employer or a tax authority, and you check it first.
  5. Lap timing. Use lap timing only on closed circuits, never on public roads.
  6. Your adapter and car. Plug in and use the adapter as its maker describes. The adapter stays powered in the OBD-II port; unplug it if the car will stand still for days.
